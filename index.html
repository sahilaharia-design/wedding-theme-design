About this book

This is a small book about a wedding. It begins with our story — how we met, what Kinara means to us, and why we chose it as the thread for the entire celebration.

In the middle, you’ll find a chapter for each of our four events: what it’s about, when it’s happening, and how to dress for it. Then a schedule, the venue, and a few things to do while you’re with us.

Towards the end, there’s a section for the wedding team and the design team — the typography, the symbols, the colours, and the small rules that hold this together. You’re welcome to read it. It’s where the work shows.

Typography

The four fonts we use across every printed and digital piece. Each one has a specific job; they don’t overlap.

The signature script · Allura Used only on hero moments — hashtag, chapter titles, monograms

#SakshiKoMilaKinara

Use sparingly. Allura is for high-emotion moments only — never for body copy, menus, or logistics. The rarer it appears, the more it carries.

The body serif · Cormorant Garamond Names, invitation copy, all readable English content

Sakshi & Dr. Sahil

The workhorse serif. Names, invitation copy, menu items, paragraphs. Use weights 300 and 400. Italic is allowed for emphasis. Avoid weights heavier than 500.

The Hindi companion · Tiro Devanagari Hindi For Hindi names, blessings, and one-liners

When Hindi appears next to English, set it slightly smaller than its Latin pairing. Same dignity, different weight on the page.

16 For our wedding team

Symbols & motifs

The small graphic marks that will appear across invites, menu cards, signage, hampers, and stationery. Five marks, each with a specific job.

Motif 01 · Where you’ll see it: dividers

The shore line

A single curve. The horizon — the line where one thing becomes another.

Used as a section divider on invites, menus, and the brand book. Appears under the hashtag, between paragraphs, and at the foot of every hamper tag. Always horizontal, always in garnet, always the same stroke weight.

Motif 02 · Where you’ll see it: invite corners, menu borders, hamper ribbons

The bloom sprig

One sprig, four colourways — one for each chapter.

The same sprig skeleton, re-coloured per event: sage-led for Bloom (Mehendi), garnet for Promise (Engagement), coral for Romance (Haldi), ivory-with-garnet for Legacy (Wedding). Tells guests which event they’re looking at without a word.

Motif 03 · Where you’ll see it: save-the-date, Mehendi pieces only

The hill

A quiet nod to where the story began — Bir.

Used only on the save-the-date and on Mehendi-chapter pieces. Never appears on Engagement, Haldi, or Wedding stationery. Rarity gives it meaning.

Motif 04 · Where you’ll see it: every signed surface

The monogram family

Five variations of ‘S & Dr. S’, each suited to a different application.

Use the seal for wax stamps and envelope closures; the inline for invite headers and letterheads; the duet for menu cards and hamper tags; the stacked for vertical banners and the welcome wall; the pattern for gift wrap and step-and-repeat backdrops.

19 For the design team

Notes for the team

A short list of rules every vendor should know before they start. Plain English — the small things that hold the whole wedding together.

i

One palette across all four events

Don’t treat each event as a separate palette. The seven core colours are shared; only the lead colour shifts per event. Decor for one event should never feel disconnected from the rest.

ii

Use the script font sparingly

Allura is for hero moments only — the hashtag, chapter titles, the monogram. Never use it in body copy, menus, schedules, or addresses. Rarity is what makes it feel emotional.

iii

Hindi sits beside English, slightly smaller

When Hindi appears next to English on any piece, set it one size smaller than its Latin pairing. Same dignity, different weight on the page.

iv

Decor stays lighter than dress code

For Engagement and Haldi, the room should be lighter than what the guests are wearing. Their jewel tones and corals will pop against ivory and peach. Never compete with the guests. (Mehendi is the exception — daytime, casual, sage-rich is fine.)

v

The hill is rationed

Use the Hill illustration only on save-the-dates and Mehendi pieces. It does not belong on the Engagement, Haldi, or Wedding stationery. It only works because it’s rare.

vi

Horizon gold is the only accent

The gold is for the hashtag, the ampersand, the foil on an envelope, the ribbon tie on a hamper. Never use it as a fill, never use it in body text. It’s reserved for moments that should glint.

vii

Leave more whitespace than you think you need

Every piece — card, menu, signage — should leave double the margins a vendor instinctively wants to use. The wedding will be loud enough. The paper should be quiet.

viii

Always write ‘Sakshi & Dr. Sahil’ in full

Never abbreviate to ‘S & S’ in running copy. Never drop the ‘Dr.’. Full names on every invite, menu, sign, and thank-you card. The monogram is the only place where initials are allowed.
